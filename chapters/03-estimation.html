🎯

Estimate

  • A prediction of how long it will take.
  • Comes with uncertainty.
"Probably 5 to 8 weeks."
📌

Target

  • A business goal or desired date.
  • Can exist before any estimate.
"We'd like it by the June launch."
🤝

Commitment

  • A promise to deliver by a date.
  • Set after comparing estimate and target.
"We commit to June 15 with this scope."

Distinction from Steve McConnell, Software Estimation (2006). Negotiate the commitment, never the estimate.

Three-point estimation (PERT)

Ask for three numbers instead of one. Adjust them and watch the range.

Triangular mean = (O + M + P) ÷ 3
PERT mean = (O + 4M + P) ÷ 6
Standard deviation σ ≈ (P − O) ÷ 6

68% / 95% ranges assume a roughly normal distribution. Treat them as approximations.

🛟 Where the safety margin goes

🙈

Hidden padding

  • Silently added to each task.
  • Gets used up anyway (Parkinson's law).
  • Destroys trust when found.
📋

Contingency reserve

  • Visible time or money for identified risks.
  • Part of the schedule and cost baselines.
  • The PM manages it.
🏦

Management reserve

  • For unknown unknowns.
  • Outside the baseline.
  • Release needs sponsor approval.

Reserve terms per PMI's PMBOK Guide. Also worth knowing: #NoEstimates teams slice work into small items and forecast from how many they finish per week (throughput).

🎯 Key takeaways

  • An estimate is a range with a confidence level, not a date.
  • Estimate ≠ target ≠ commitment. Negotiate the commitment, not the estimate.
  • Re-estimate as uncertainty shrinks, and keep buffers visible.
