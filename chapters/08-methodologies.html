💡 Learn

Two families of approaches

PMBOK 7 calls them predictive and adaptive. Most real projects mix both.

📐 Predictive (waterfall)

Plan the whole thing up front, then execute in phases.
Req→Design→Build→Test→Release
  • Phase gates: approve before moving on
  • Scope fixed early; change goes through change control
  • Fits: stable or regulated scope, hardware, construction, fixed-price contracts
  • Risk: feedback arrives late, when change is expensive

🔁 Adaptive (agile)

Deliver in small increments and adjust as you learn.
Plan→Build→Review→Adapt↺
  • Short iterations or continuous flow
  • Scope evolves; time and team often fixed
  • Fits: uncertain needs, new products, fast feedback available
  • Risk: drifting without a clear goal or a product owner

Tailoring factors (PMBOK 7)

What PMBOK 7 says to weigh when choosing the development approach.

📦 The product

  • Degree of innovation
  • Requirements certainty
  • Scope stability
  • Ease of change
  • Delivery options
  • Risk, safety, regulation

🗂️ The project

  • Stakeholders and their availability
  • Schedule constraints
  • Funding availability

🏢 The organization

  • Structure and culture
  • Team capability
  • Team size and location

🎯 Key takeaways

  • No method is best. Uncertainty decides: stable scope suits predictive, unknowns suit agile.
  • Agile still plans and still has deadlines. It plans more often, in smaller steps.
  • Most teams run hybrid. Tailor deliberately and say why.
