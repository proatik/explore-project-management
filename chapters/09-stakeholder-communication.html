💡 Plan

Plan who hears what

A communication plan answers: who, what, why, how, how often, and who sends it. Pick a channel and frequency for each stakeholder.

Mapping stakeholders by power and interest is covered in Chapter 01. This plan builds on that map.

Push, pull, interactive

PMBOK names three ways to communicate. Good plans mix all three.

📤

Push

  • You send it to specific people
  • Email, weekly status, release notes
  • Sent ≠ read ≠ understood
📚

Pull

  • People fetch it when they need it
  • Wiki, dashboard, decision log
  • Great for large or occasional audiences
💬

Interactive

  • Two-way, in real time
  • Meetings, calls, live chat
  • Best for bad news, conflict, decisions

    Status

    Include

    What the colors mean

    Agree on definitions up front. These are common ones; your company may differ.

    🟢 Green

    • On track for scope, date and budget
    • No help needed

    🟠 Amber

    • At risk, but the team has a recovery plan
    • Leaders should watch, may need to help

    🔴 Red

    • Off track without outside help
    • Needs a decision or support now

    🍉 Watermelon status

    • Green outside, red inside
    • Reports stay green until the week it's too late
    • Cause: people fear that red means blame
    • Fix: treat red as a request for help, not a failure

    🏷️ Label every item

    • DECISIONsomeone must choose, by a date
    • ASKyou need help or resources
    • FYIno action needed
    • Readers scan for their name and these labels

    • Tailor length, detail and channel to each reader. Keep the facts the same.
