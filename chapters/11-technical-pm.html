Architecture Decision Records

A short doc per significant decision (Michael Nygard's format).

ADR-014 · Example
Title
Use PostgreSQL for the orders service
Status
Accepted
Context
Orders need transactions and joins. The team already runs Postgres.
Decision
Store orders in PostgreSQL, not the document store.
Consequences
Strong consistency. Schema migrations need planning.

Why a TPM cares

  • Decisions stop being re-argued every quarter
  • New joiners learn the "why" in minutes
  • Consequences become planned work, not surprises
  • Superseded, never deleted: history stays

SLIs, SLOs and error budgets

From Google's Site Reliability Engineering book: reliability as a number you can plan with.

SLI
Indicator

What you measure. e.g. % of requests served under 300 ms.

SLO
Objective

Your internal target. e.g. 99.9% over 30 days.

SLA
Agreement

A promise to customers, with penalties. Looser than the SLO.

DORA: the four keys

From Google's DevOps Research and Assessment program. Two for speed, two for stability.

SpeedDeployment frequency

How often you ship to production.

💬 "What stops us shipping daily?"
SpeedLead time for changes

Commit to running in production.

💬 "Where does work wait?"
StabilityChange failure rate

% of deployments that cause a failure.

💬 "What do our tests miss?"
StabilityTime to restore

How fast you restore service after a production failure. Newer reports narrow it to failed deployment recovery time.

💬 "Can we roll back in minutes?"

Speed and stability move together in high performers: small, frequent changes are safer.

🎯 Key takeaways

  • Your job is the seams: contracts, dependencies and shared dates between teams.
  • Make risk small: feature flags, canaries and fast rollback beat big-bang launches.
  • Plan with numbers: error budgets and DORA metrics turn "is it stable?" into data.
